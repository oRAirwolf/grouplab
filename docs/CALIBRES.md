# Calibres in GroupLab

The calibre is entered as a cartridge name or as the bullet's diameter. Generated by `grouplab calibres` from `src/GroupLab.Core/Marking/Calibre.cs`; do not edit it by hand, because a test fails when this file differs from what the command writes.

## 1. How to enter a calibre

Type the cartridge's name, such as 6.5 Creedmoor, 308 or 9mm, or the bullet's diameter in inches, such as .264 or 0.264, or in millimetres with mm, such as 6.71 mm. **A name is matched before a number** (entry 163 section 3): 6.5 is the 6.5 mm family, 0.264 in, and not 6.5 mm, and .38 is 0.357 in, and not 0.380. As you type, GroupLab lists the cartridges it knows with their diameter beside them, the common first, and under the first it names the family you could pick by mistake.

Only what matches no name is read as a diameter. A designation that names no family in the table is refused rather than read as a diameter, because it is a name written as a number: 7.62 mm could be three different bullets. The designations refused when they name no family are, in inches, .17, .20, .22, .25, .27, .28, .30, .303, .32, .35, .38, .44 and .45, and in millimetres, 5.45 mm, 5.56 mm, 6 mm, 6.5 mm, 6.8 mm, 7 mm, 7.5 mm, 7.62 mm, 7.65 mm, 8 mm, 9 mm and 10 mm. A bare number of one or more that names nothing, such as 7.62 or 22, is refused for the same reason. Every diameter must lie between 0.1 and 1 in.

A designation's refusal names the problem, for example: "7.62 mm is a calibre's name, not the bullet's diameter. Enter the bullet's diameter, such as 7.82 mm or 0.308."

A refusal says what to type: "Enter the bullet diameter in inches, such as 0.308, or in millimetres with mm, such as 7.82 mm."

## 5. Why names came back

Alan decided in entry 107 that the calibre is a diameter and nothing else, reversing entries 105 and 106, which read names through a table whose defects all came from reading a name as a diameter. Entry 108 refused the designations a person might type as a diameter. **Entry 163 reverses both, on the evidence of the first real user**: a person who shoots a 6.5 Creedmoor typed 6.5 and was offered .257, because the pick list matched 6.53 mm. A name he knows is a better thing to ask for than a diameter he does not, provided the table is right, which is why every name in it has two sources and every disagreement between them keeps the name out.
